01

Hedging and commodity-price exposure

Hedging begins with the underlying position: the quantity exposed, the pricing period, the contractual reference and the timing of cash flows. A proposed hedge should be assessed against the physical transaction rather than selected simply because an instrument is available.

Consultation can consider fixed versus floating pricing, staged price protection, timing differences and basis risk—the risk that the hedge reference and the physical material do not move together. An approach that suits one commodity or contract may not suit another.

Implementation, where appropriate, requires suitable instruments, counterparty arrangements, documentation and approvals. These are assessed separately from the initial exposure review.

02

Foreign exchange solutions

Currency movements can change the economics of purchase costs, sales proceeds, freight and financing. The first step is to identify which currencies matter and when each exposure is created or settled.

Our FX focus connects potential protection approaches to the trade’s payment schedule, contractual currency and commercial objectives. The purpose is to understand and manage exposure, not to promise exchange-rate outcomes.

03

Deep market consultation

A market discussion should answer a commercial question. Relevant topics can include supply and demand, regional market conditions, product quality, processing routes, substitution, delivery constraints and the implications of changing market conditions.

The scope can range from a focused question on a proposed transaction to a wider review of the material and market assumptions behind a commercial programme.

04

Benchmarks and pricing references

A quoted benchmark is a reference, not a complete description of a specific material. Its location, currency, unit, quotation timing and product basis may differ from the proposed trade.

Benchmark consultation considers those differences and their significance for pricing, settlement and risk. This is particularly important when quality adjustments, treatment terms or other differentials carry a meaningful part of the transaction’s economics.

05

What to bring

Outline the commercial decision, the product, the pricing or currency exposure and the relevant timing. If a contract is involved, the pricing and payment clauses will usually be more useful than a general market forecast.
